## Concept
Horror delivery game. Players load a delivery van (the "Graveyard Express") at a warehouse start area, then drive
it down a forest road to deliver packages to houses.

## Systems
- **Delivery chute** (`ServerScriptService.DeliveryChute`): when the van's rear doors are open and the chute mouth is
  inside the cargo area, a box spawns every 1.2s up to 24. Landed boxes are welded to `Cargo.Floor` after 3s or
  destroyed if they missed. Status lamp: red = no van, orange = doors closed, green = loading, blue = full.
- **Shops** (`ServerScriptService.StartAreaShops`): triggering a `ShopPrompt` fires `ReplicatedStorage.Remotes.OpenShop`
  to that client with the ShopId. **The shop UI is not built yet** (no client listener).
- **Framework** (`ServerScriptService.Server`): Bootstrap, Core (ServiceList, Scheduler, Net), Services (DataService,
  MonetizationService, ReplicationService, AntiExploitService). Persistence via `ProfileStore`. Admin via `Cmdr`.
  Shared code in `ReplicatedStorage.Shared` (Loader, Enums, Types, Util, NetDefs, Config).


## Planned: Core Loop
- Each shift is a list of deliveries. The van's in-cab `TrackerScreen` runs a real-time **Heading-Up GPS radar** (van chevron fixed pointing up, rotating `N / E / S / W` compass labels, rotating radar sweep): the current active delivery house appears as a bright pulsing **green dot**, remaining shift stops appear as smaller/dimmer green dots, and once deliveries finish or time expires the Next Factory Depot appears as a pulsing **gold dot**.
- The player is always behind schedule, and every monster threat costs time, so they choose between speed and safety.
- **Endless Forward Factory Depot Progression:** Instead of teleporting back to `(0, 0)` when a shift ends, both finishing all deliveries AND running out of time (`0:00`, forfeiting the on-time bonus) spawn/relocate the Factory Depot further down the continuing procedural road onto a reserved roadside lot (`~6–8` cells ahead) with a glowing green bay beacon. Players drive to the new roadside depot to collect shift payout, restock boxes at the chute, and browse shops. An invisible collision wall paired with subtle weathered industrial sawhorse barricades, blinking amber hazard beacons, and traffic cones blocks continuing further down the road until **every currently alive player** clicks the small **"Start Next Shift"** HUD ready-up button (automatically handling deaths, respawns, and disconnects).
- At the end of each shift the player is paid based on deliveries completed and time taken, then spends the money on van upgrades.

## Planned: Procedural Map
- The map is generated as the player drives. Roads, intersections and houses spawn ahead of the van; old chunks behind it despawn or are pooled to stay memory-safe.
- Uses the existing HorrorKit models in `ReplicatedStorage.Assets.MapModels` (Houses, Infrastructure, Props, Roads). Do not generate placeholder models; ask for missing ones.
- **Multi-Path Looping Road Network & Advanced Macro Formations:** Uses 11 active HorrorKit road templates (`Road_Straight`, `Road_Cracked`, `Road_Turn90`, `Road_TIntersection`, plus the 7 Blender road models in `Assets/Roads/`: `Road_Bridge`, `Road_PulloffBay`, `Road_DamagedRough`, `Road_CrossIntersection`, `Road_Roundabout`, `Road_SCurve`, `Road_WideTurn90`; `Road_CulDeSac` is excluded so there are zero dead ends). Randomly rotates through 6 distinct 100%-looping macro formations (preventing back-to-back repeats) while keeping all actual delivery stop houses strictly on straight main-spine cells and using existing `HorrorKit_Houses` (`House_A_Cabin`, `House_B_Suburban`, `House_C_Ranch`) strictly as non-delivery decorative houses with yard fences/mailboxes along side branches:
  1. **Deep-Forest Wing ("Out-and-Back Sweep"):** Shoots `4–5` cells (`400–500` studs) sideways deep into the dense woods around sweeping `Road_WideTurn90` bends and returns straight to the main road.
  2. **Staggered / Asymmetrical "Z-Split":** Offset dual-side branches where one side splits early and rejoins mid-way while the opposite side splits 3 cells later and rejoins further north.
  3. **Ghost-Town "Crossroads Village Block":** Compact `5×5` mini-neighborhood built around alternating `Road_Roundabout` and `Road_CrossIntersection` hubs with parallel side streets, 3 cross-alleys, streetlamps, and `4–6` decorative abandoned houses.
  4. **Long Forest Backroad with Mid-Way Bailouts:** `9`-cell-long forest route `300` studs out with sweeping `Road_WideTurn90` bends, isolated cabins, and a mid-way cross-connector bridge lane back to the main spine.
  5. **Double-Roundabout "Figure-8 / S-Hub":** Two asymmetric dual-loop roundabout lobes separated by a center `Road_Bridge` straight stretch with alternating wide (`3`-cell) and tight (`2`-cell) sweeping outer arcs.
  6. **Sweeping S-Curve Chicane Split:** Fast curvy outer route chaining `Road_WideTurn90` + `Road_SCurve` + `Road_WideTurn90` against a shorter `Road_DamagedRough` / `Road_Cracked` inner spine road.
- **Dense Deep Forest & Map Containment:** Surrounding forest extends 3 cells (`300` studs) deep on every side of the road and grows progressively denser and taller farther from the road (`~0.88x–1.12x` roadside → `~1.50x–2.00x` deep woods; gently trimmed to `4–6` / `8–11` / `12–16` trees per chunk across Rings 1–3 and `175` around the starting factory for smoother FPS without looking empty) using 8 low-poly dead tree variants (`Prop_DeadTree_A/B` + `DeadTree_01_Fork`..`DeadTree_06_Crook`, source files in `Assets/Props/DeadTrees.blend` & `DeadTrees.glb`). Near-road trees (`< 95` studs from road center) remain non-collidable so the van never snags; deeper forest trees (`>= 95` studs) are solid blockers and outer Ring 3 edges are sealed with invisible `ForestBoundary` walls to slow down and contain players who wander off-road.
- **Creeping Trailing Dark-Fog & Debris Barrier ("No Turning Back"):** As the van advances down the procedural road, a creeping `BackFogBarrier` (~10–11 cells / ~1,000–1,200 studs behind `maxReachedSpine` & `maxReachedGz`, always safely ahead of chunk despawn and clamped ≥8 cells behind the active Factory Depot while the shift gate is up) seals the entire map corridor East-West (`3,200` studs wide across two `1,600`-stud halves spanning well past both forest boundaries so nobody can drive around it) with an invisible collision wall, layered vertical-gradient dark fog curtains (`UIGradient` seamlessly dissolving into the night sky), billowing dark `ParticleEmitter` mist clouds, fallen dead trees, and broken guardrail/fence/chevron debris (no HUD/radar bump warning — purely atmospheric). Chunks between the barrier and the van never despawn, and leftover `StartAreaGround` is removed when the Factory Depot relocates. If the player drives past an active delivery house and the trailing fog swallows it, that stop is automatically marked **MISSED** (forfeiting its \$100 payout and shift on-time bonus) and the shift advances immediately to the next stop (or Next Factory Depot) so it can never soft-lock.
- Generation is seeded so a shift can be reproduced, and always keeps at least one valid route to the current delivery address.
- Delivery addresses are placed on houses that exist in the generated map; the tracker screen updates as new sections generate.
- Generation must stay compatible with monsters (e.g. avoid dead ends right behind the player so the chaser has a path).

## Planned: Monsters (each forces a different action)
1. **Window monster (`Monster2_Window`):** pops up at the left or right window. Player must close the window or the driver cannot see.
2. **Stare monster (`Monster3_Stare`):** appears in the rear-view mirror or on the passenger seat. Player must look at it until it vanishes, but looking away from the road makes the van drift.
3. **Light/screen gremlin (`Monster4_Gremlin`):** small crouched shadow-gremlin that perches on the hood/wipers or clings near dashboard electronics to kill headlights or the tracker screen. Player must fix it (a switch or a camera feed) while navigating blind.
4. **Chaser (Colossal Broken-Legged Skinwalker Crawler / `Monster6_Skinwalker`):** a huge, broken-legged crawler dragging itself behind the van via environment-grabbing IK arms; spawns **only** if players stand still for too long (`22s`), take too long without delivering (`70s` or `<= 35s` shift time left), or finish the shift with undelivered packages (`Return` phase with incomplete stops). Gets closer when the player slows down or crashes, falls back when they use Boost `[Q]` or `[H]` Horn flinch.
5. **Cargo monster (`Monster5_Cargo`):** twisted, spider-like stowaway crawler that sneaks into the back of the van. Player must check the rear camera and lock the cargo door, or packages get wrecked.
6. **Skinwalker (`Monster6_Skinwalker`):** uncanny humanoid/deer-like hybrid that stands motionless in the middle of the road to bait drivers into swerving—or transforms into a relentless stalker if hit.
7. **Deep-forest / Far-from-van hunter *(planned, not built yet)*:** kills players who stray too far away from the delivery van into the woods; pairs with the dense, solid deep forest designed to slow off-road players down.
8. **Door-Ripper (`Monster3_DoorRipper` / `DoorMonster`):** driver-side roadside hijacker that sprints out of the dark shoulder while the van is moving (`>= 24` studs/s), leaps onto the left running board, and pries the driver door open. The driver must mash `[E]` / `[X]` to pull the door shut (`DoorMash` remote) — slamming it shut knocks the monster tumbling down the road, whereas failing lets it rip the driver out onto the asphalt (`-35` HP, `-10s` shift penalty) while the driverless van keeps rolling forward (`VanCoastUntil`).

## Planned: Upgrades (bought between shifts)
- Better tracker screen (shows the next two stops, flags danger zones)
- Windshield and window covers (protect against the window monster)
- Headlights and horn (the horn can scare off some monsters)
- Engine and tires (speed and grip)
- Rear camera and cargo lock (counter the cargo monster)
- Flashlight upgrades (`ProductCatalog.BetterFlashlight` already exists)

## Planned: Optional Co-op
Two players: a driver and a navigator. The navigator sees the map and calls out turns while the driver deals with monsters. Solo play uses the tracker screen as the navigator.
